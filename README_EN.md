# Digital Millennium

🌐 [中文](README.md)

## 🧭 What This Is

A repair worker can hear that a machine is going wrong before a gauge shows a fault. A nurse knows what must not be left out during handover. A teacher notices the moment a student stops following. A farmer reads weather and soil before deciding whether to act. An office worker may sense that a project is at risk from what a meeting leaves unsaid.

Much of this knowledge never reaches a book. It lives in movements, judgments, sequences, exceptions, and instincts built through repeated work. When someone changes jobs, retires, or dies, the knowledge may have no way to reach the next person. It is not ranked by occupation, industry, job title, education, language, or country. People who work with their hands, people who study and write, and people who care for, teach, build, organize, repair, grow, or create should all be able to share their experience and use what comes from it.

Sharing and using this knowledge should not depend on one's profession. We want knowledge from every kind of work to become a shared commons, shaped and used by workers, intellectuals, and practitioners everywhere.

Digital Millennium is an early, open effort to give these experiences a place where they can gradually be described, structured, tested through use, corrected, and turned into Skills that people can read and AI can use. A contributor does not need to know how to write a complete Skill before their experience has value.

This repository is infrastructure for that work. It is not another Skill warehouse or a Skill download site. A Skill is one possible result of describing, structuring, testing, and revising experience. It is not the project itself.

## 🧩 How a Skill Takes Shape

A Skill may begin with a work experience, a book, a study, a standard, or a method. The first step is to make a judgment that usually stays in one person's head easier to describe. Conditions, steps, exceptions, and sources can then be organized into something another person can read. Actual use exposes omissions and misunderstandings, and that feedback informs the next revision. A Skill is not a document written once and left untouched. It is a knowledge tool tested by use.

The protocol, format, and taxonomy are still taking shape. Nothing here should be treated as final. Early review will remain lightweight, focusing on the specification, format, and declaration of sources. There will be no complex committee or voting system at this stage. The point is not to pretend that judgment is already perfect, but to let material reach real use and let problems improve the rules.

## 🌱 The Early Stage

The project is currently establishing its first reference Skill and refining the standard through real use and feedback. The contribution flow, protocol, and taxonomy are not finalized.

If you know how a judgment is made on the job but do not yet know how to write a complete Skill, that is enough to begin. Bring a situation, an experience, or a practice worth testing. Its limits, wording, and missing pieces can become clearer through later organization and use.

## 🗺️ A Possible Future Matrix

As the body of work grows, the project may use an extensible matrix to connect Skills. One dimension could describe application domains, while another could describe cognitive and action capabilities such as understanding, judgment, planning, or execution. This direction still needs to be tested against real material. It is not a completed taxonomy.

## 👀 Follow Along

You can look at the reference Skill as it takes shape, join the discussion, or suggest a piece of practical experience worth developing. The project does not require every rule to be finished before work begins. The rules and the material can become clearer together, through use and feedback.
